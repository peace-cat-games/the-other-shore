# Righteous Among the Nations from Denmark and Sweden

Research notes for "The Other Shore: October 1943", for the closing roll of honour. Every name below comes from Yad Vashem: its per-country lists and the records in its Righteous Database. Each was fetched and read on 8 November 2026. Nothing here was filled in from memory or from Wikipedia. Wikipedia and other secondary sites were used only as pointers, and the places where they go wrong are listed at the end.

**The short version**

- Denmark: **22** people, in 15 files. Sweden: **10** people, in 9 files. These are Yad Vashem's official figures "as of January 1, 2024" [S1]. The per-country PDFs [S2][S3] and the live database [S6] give the same totals, and both lists below match them exactly. No name is missing.
- Most Danish rescuers have no individual title, because the Danish underground asked not to be honoured one by one. Yad Vashem planted a tree for the Danish underground instead [S1][S7].
- **None of the 10 Swedish Righteous was recognized for the 1943 rescue of the Danish Jews.** They acted in Budapest, Berlin, Rome and Genoa.
- Georg Ferdinand Duckwitz, who acted in Copenhagen, is listed under **Germany**, not Denmark [S4][S30].

**How to print the names.** Use the "Name" column. It turns Yad Vashem's list entry (surname first, shown in the next column) into normal word order, and it names every person. Keep Yad Vashem's spellings, including the unusual ones flagged in the last section. The Danish and Swedish names on these lists contain no æ, ø, å, ä or ö. Yad Vashem's database writes Danish place names in plain ASCII ("Hong" for Høng, "Espergaerde" for Espergærde, "Koege" for Køge). The table below uses the Danish spellings that Yad Vashem's own narrative uses where it has them.

## 2. Denmark

### 2a. Why the Danish list is short: the collective commemoration

What Yad Vashem says, in its own words:

- Statistics page, the asterisk on "Denmark * 22": "The title of Righteous is awarded to individuals, not to groups. The members of the Danish resistance viewed the rescue operation as a collective act and therefore asked Yad Vashem not to recognize resistance members individually. Yad Vashem respected their request and consequently the number of Danish Righteous is relatively small. A tree was planted on the Mount of Remembrance to commemorate the Danish resistance." [S1]
- Yad Vashem's "Related Sites" page, section "The Tree in Honor of the Danish Underground", which also mentions a plaque: "To pay tribute to this exceptional rescue operation and in the understanding that this was a joint effort, a tree was planted in the Avenue of the Righteous in honor of the Danish underground. The Underground did not receive the Righteous Among the Nations title, which is only awarded to individuals, not to groups, This was also in the spirit of the request expressed by the members of the Danish underground not to honor them as individuals. Nevertheless, several Danes, whose acts of rescue were exceptional, were awarded the title of Righteous Among the Nations title." [S7] (The live page now returns 404. The quotation is from the Wayback copy of 4 August 2024.)
- The same page adds that "since the 1990's ... Danish scholars have shown that in many cases big sums of money were paid to the seamen who brought the Jews across to Sweden". It gives 7,200 Jews and "some 700 of their non-Jewish relatives" brought to safety, and 482 Jews caught and deported to Theresienstadt [S7].
- Yad Vashem's 2013 blog post on the 70th anniversary has nearly the same wording about the tree in the Avenue of the Righteous and the underground's wish. It also says that the Danish ambassador "paid tribute to the rescuers at the trees planted in honor of the Danish" [S8]. Note the plural "trees".
- Yad Vashem event page, 7 June 2016 (visit of the Speaker of the Folketing): "the tree planted in honor of the People of Denmark, who collectively rescued some 7,200 Jews and 700 of their non-Jewish relatives". It also mentions "the Righteous Among the Nations tree planted on the Mount of Remembrance in honor of the Danish people" [S9].

**Safe wording for the game.** Yad Vashem planted a tree in honour of the Danish underground, which some of its pages call the Danish people. At the underground's own request its members were not named individually, and the underground as a group does not hold the Righteous title. Do not write that "the Danish resistance was named Righteous Among the Nations" [S7][S1].

**Where the tree is.** Yad Vashem's pages say both "on the Mount of Remembrance" [S1][S9] and "in the Avenue of the Righteous" [S7][S8]. Yad Vashem stands on the Mount of Remembrance, so these probably describe the same place. That is an inference; no source fetched here says so in one sentence.

**The boat.**
- Yad Vashem's photo archive (item 4613/333) describes "One of the Danish boats used in October 1943 to ferry Jews from the town of Gilleleje to fishing boats at sea, which then brought them to safety in Sweden. The boat is on permanent display at the Yad Vashem Museum in Jerusalem." [S10]
- The 2016 event page shows the visitor "In the Holocaust History Museum, at the exhibit dedicated to the rescue of Denmark's Jews by boat in October 1943" [S9].
- A Yad Vashem postcard, transcribed by a German local-history site, says that the boat carried Jews "near the town Gillelje" (sic) out to fishing boats, and that "The boat has been placed here to commemorate the 40th anniversary of the rescue of Danish Jewry" [S11]. That would be 1983, but no Yad Vashem page fetched here gives the year. Treat the 40th-anniversary detail as secondary.
- The boat is a memorial exhibit. It is not a Righteous honour.

## 6. Unverified or uncertain

**Spelling and date differences inside Yad Vashem's own sources.** Settle each one before printing.

- **Gunnerso / "Gunners".** The Denmark PDF prints "Gunners, Pastor Arnold & Karen" [S2]. The database's surname field and narrative both say "Gunnerso" ("the Gunnersos") [S15], and so does holocaustrescue.org [S42]. "Gunners" looks like a truncation in the PDF. The Danish original may be "Gunnersø", but no source found says so, so don't print "Gunnersø" unless one turns up. **Recommendation: print "Gunnerso".** That is Yad Vashem's database spelling, and its narrative uses it too.
- **Fredrik / Frederik Fogh.** The PDF and the database name field say "Fredrik". The narrative says "Frederik" [S2][S14]. Print "Fredrik".
- **Henry Christian / "Christen" Thomsen.** The database and narrative say "Henry Christian" [S22]. JFR says "Henry Christen" [S38]. The PDF prints only "Henry" [S2]. Print "Henry" or "Henry Christian"; not "Christen".
- **Carl Iwan / Ivan Danielsson.** The PDF and the database name field say "Iwan". The narrative says "Ivan" [S3][S29]. Swedish Wikipedia notes he is "stavat Iwan hos Yad Vashem" [S39]. Print "Carl Iwan".
- **Duckwitz's first names.** The Germany PDF reads "Duckwitz, Ferdinand Georg" [S4]. The database and narrative say "Georg Ferdinand" [S30]. Print "Georg Ferdinand Duckwitz".
- **Knud Dyby.** The record is headed "Dyby (Dyring), Knud". Dyring was his name before the war [S13]. Print "Knud Dyby".
- **Marstrand Christiansen.** The PDF says "Marstrand Christiansen, Knud & Karen". The database stores the surname as "Marstrand; Christiansen". The closing line of the narrative says "Knud Christiansen Marstrand and Karen Christiansen Marstrand" [S2][S18]. JFR says "Knud Christiansen" [S38]. Print "Knud & Karen Marstrand Christiansen", as in the PDF.
- **Per Anger's year.** The PDF says 1980 [S3]. The database date and the narrative say 28 April 1981 [S27]. Swedish Wikipedia follows the PDF [S39]. If a year is printed, say "1981 (listed by Yad Vashem as 1980)", or print no year.
- **Erik Perwe's date.** The database field says 23 October 2006. The narrative says "On 23 June 2006" [S35]. The year, 2006, is consistent.
- **Harald Petersen's place.** The database place-of-rescue field reads "Aachen Burtscheid, Germany" [S20]. His narrative is entirely in Denmark, and the database lists his main rescue place as Denmark [S6]. This looks like a database error. Use the narrative.

**Near misses: not on either list, so don't print them as Danish or Swedish Righteous.**

- **Sigrid Helliesen-Lund.** The database gives her nationality as "NORWAY; SWEDEN" (her mother was Swedish). She rescued Jews in Oslo and helped smuggle children to Sweden. Yad Vashem lists her under **Norway** (file 10856, 2006) [S5][S37], and she is not counted in Sweden's 10.
- **Manuel Antonio Muñoz Borrero** (Ecuador, 2011; place of rescue Stockholm) and **Karl & Inga Trosholmen (Solbergseter) and son Asbjörn** (Norway, 2014; place of rescue "Sweden"; the Norway PDF spells the name "Trosholem") also come up in the database [S6][S5]. Neither is Swedish, and neither has anything to do with Denmark. Their records were not read in full.
- **Swedish Wikipedia** also names Alexander Erba-Odescalchi and Kazimir Vilnis as "Swedish-connected" Righteous recognized under other countries [S39]. This was not checked against Yad Vashem, and they are not on the Swedish list.

**Errors in secondary sources. Don't repeat these.**

- **Danish Wikipedia** says that the members of the resistance who took part in the rescue are "opført som en gruppe bestående af 22 personer" [S40]. That is wrong. The 22 are individual Danish Righteous, and the underground holds no title [S1][S7].
- **English Wikipedia** says that in Denmark "a group of people was recognized as a single entity" [S41]. Yad Vashem says that the underground "did not receive the Righteous Among the Nations title" [S7].
